Methods · computational techniques

Techniques behind the maps and graphs

Catholic Media of the Americas is scored with classical IR, deterministic lexicon matching, rate normalization, association measures, and graph construction, not opaque embeddings. Each technique below maps to a visible claim on Places, Topics, Findings, or Triage.

Education & impact

Why the method matters beyond the dashboard

Classroom & OCIA

Inquirers often meet the Church in news and digital media before a syllabus. Shareable lift charts and place panels give formation leaders evidence of which vocabularies already circulate in their region.

Scholarly transparency

Every claim is tied to a reproducible step: lexicon match → country rate → lift vs. baseline. Limits (ActionGeo, English-skewed wires) stay visible so students learn responsible computational theology, not black-box maps.

Force for good

Measuring liberation, preferential option, and magisterial language as data, while redacting personal identifiers, turns Notre Dame’s mission into hemispheric evidence educators can cite, critique, and extend.

Techniques

Pipeline shape: information retrieval and harvest → language/time/geo normalization → lexicon labeling → aggregation and association → static classroom viz. Multimodal sources (news, TV, radio, YouTube; X forthcoming) become text spans scored against a fixed multilingual lexicon; exports feed Places heat, Topics graphs, and Findings charts (data.js).

Controlled lexicon matching
Frozen, versioned THEME_PATTERNS (compiled regex per theme × language). Output is a binary theme set per document span, not embedding similarity and not open-ended keyword search. Parallel EN/ES/PT surface forms collapse to one teaching ID.
Geolocated IR / ActionGeo
Keyword-gated collection plus GDELT ActionGeo → FIPS aggregation. Coordinates mark writing / event location in the stream, not viewership. Metro and country layers are therefore writing geography under a shared constraint.
Rate normalization
Theme presence is reported as hits per 1,000 documents (or paragraphs) so large wire desks do not automatically dominate interpretation. Country teaching compares rates against a U.S. baseline.
Association / lift
Theme co-occurrence edges use lift P(a,b) / (P(a)·P(b)) (equivalently n·w / (na·nb)). Edges are kept by minimum lift, not raw weight, so generic high-volume terms do not flood the Topics graph.
Bipartite + undirected graphs
Country × theme bipartite networks rank teachings by lift vs. U.S.; theme–theme undirected graphs show co-travel. Language-bridge exports link parallel EN/ES/PT terms to the same concept node.
Network dependence (QAP)
Quadratic Assignment Procedure diagnostics compare expected cultural teaching maps with observed lifts (reported in corpus network exports; QAP summary appears in the classroom header when available).
Privacy pipeline
Personal identifiers are redacted before anonymized shards and classroom payloads ship: techniques stay inspectable without exposing people in the source text.
  1. Collect: keyword-gated harvest across EN/ES/PT media streams (2015–Q2 2026).
  2. Normalize: language ID, time bucketing (day/quarter), ActionGeo → FIPS, PII redaction.
  3. Label: deterministic lexicon matchers (compiled regex per theme × language).
  4. Aggregate: rates per 1k; co-occurrence matrices; lift and QAP diagnostics.
  5. Triage (Education): dual taxonomy (content + discourse) with accuracy flags; association tests feed the plain-language Triage board.
  6. Serve: static classroom site: Story, Findings, Triage, Misconceptions, Label, Places, Topics, Methods, and Next.

What the statistics can and cannot say

Estimator
A theme hit is binary on a paragraph after a frozen regex. The reported quantity is a rate per 1,000 paragraphs, or lift n·w / (na·nb) against a stated baseline (usually U.S. news). Lift above 1 means the pair is more common than independence, not that the theme is important.
Null and correction
Triage contrasts use two-sided Fisher exact tests. Holm–Bonferroni is applied inside each family (content pairs, place×type, language, news vs radio) at α = 0.05. A star on one family is not a license to scan the others.
Dependence
Paragraphs from the same outlet and day are not independent draws. Fisher p-values are therefore sharper than a design that clustered by outlet. Treat them as triage, not as a census of the Americas. Network comparisons use QAP because permuting labels respects that the graph is one object.
Selection
Collection is keyword-gated. The corpus is Catholic-media talk that matched the lexicon, not a sample of all news. Rates describe that gated stream.
Labels
Lexicon matches are deterministic. Education labels and the 0–4 accuracy score come from a model (Jev). They queue excerpts for a theologian. They are not inter-annotator gold.

Theological claims this corpus can touch

These are claims about public language, checked against writing location and lexicon or triage labels. None of them is a finding about what audiences believe.

Cuts against

One hemispheric Catholic voice

If media spoke one Catholic register, country×theme lifts would sit near the U.S. baseline. Mexico–Guadalupe, Brazil–Aparecida, and other pre-specified cells do not. That cuts against homogenization of writing. It does not show that audiences hear different churches.

Consistent with

Inculturation and popular piety are place-marked

Guadalupe and Aparecida are coded as devotion tied to a place, not as a generic Marian token. Their lifts on the pre-specified map match that expectation for ActionGeo news. The match was specified before coloring the cells; it is not a search over every saint name.

Consistent with

Gutiérrez: the option for the poor leaves a public footprint

The liberation-theology lexicon is the test: higher prevalence in Latin American news than in U.S. coverage, as the Story page reports. That is a keyword rate by writing location. It does not show the theology is practiced more, or that Gutiérrez caused the coverage.

Consistent with

News covers the Church as an institution

In the Education triage set, institutional news is about 32% of excerpts and doctrine about 3%. Inside news text the institutional share is about 38%. Radio is about 5%. That fits a press habit of covering offices and events more than creed. It does not show doctrine is absent from parishes, or that secularization caused the mix.

Not tested

People learn the faith from media before OCIA

Mediatization as a claim about learners needs audiences, syllabi, or interviews. This corpus has texts and a writing location. Keyword gates also mean we never see Catholic life that never uses these words.

Informal-learning triage (Education)

Beyond lexicon geography, the Education layer labels anonymized excerpts with two orthogonal multi-label schemes: what media may teach before OCIA/parish formation (doctrine, CST, devotion, moral, migration/politics, mission, misunderstanding, and related types) and how it is said (question, news report, claim, advocacy, homily, interview, and related acts). An accuracy score (0–4) flags rows for human and Theology review. It is a listening aid for educators, not a magisterial judgment of the Church.

How associations are tested
Place accents, theme co-travel, language mix, and news-vs-radio contrasts use Fisher exact tests with Holm–Bonferroni correction within each family (α = 0.05). On the Triage page those results appear as plain badges (Strong / Clear / Modest / Unclear), not star counts.
Connection maps
Educators see a bipartite map of places and the teaching types they elevate, plus a theme-co-travel view. Clicking a circle or line yields a plain-language readout. Interactive figures with short EN/ES/PT vignettes sit below the lists.

Lexicon (definition)

A lexicon here is a frozen, versioned set of controlled vocabulary patterns: the labeling technique above, spelled out as data. Each entry is a named teaching (theme ID) plus one or more language-specific regular expressions that fire on token and phrase boundaries. Hits are binary per document span: the theme is present or not.

The list is not only mission verbs. It includes papal / magisterial document titles (encyclicals and exhortations such as Rerum Novarum, Laudato Si', Fratelli Tutti, Evangelii Gaudium), Catholic social teaching umbrella phrases, regional and popular-piety markers (Guadalupe, Aparecida, Romero), and parallel EN/ES/PT evangelization terms. Pontiff names enter mainly through those document titles and CST phrases rather than as bare name tokens. A broader collection keyword gate (used to harvest media) sits beside the scoring patterns and is included in the CSV.

Parallel teachings
English, Spanish, and Portuguese surface forms collapse to one analytic teaching (e.g. gospel / evangelio / evangelho). The UI search box accepts all three; the graph node is singular.
Theme groups
Patterns are bucketed for navigation: core mission & evangelization, pastoral formation & catechesis, cultural & social context, and theological accents (magisterial, liberation / preferential option, popular piety, sacramental, inculturation, synodal).
What a hit is
Matcher output is a set of theme IDs per text unit. We do not treat raw mention counts as belief intensity; we use them as features for rates and associations. Gold-standard human labels are planned to calibrate precision/recall.
Examples (illustrative)
liberation_theology ← “liberation theology” · “teología de la liberación” · “teologia da libertação”; preferential_option ← “option for the poor” · “opción preferencial”; laudato_si / fratelli_tutti / evangelii_gaudium ← encyclical & exhortation titles across languages.

Scoring uses a frozen THEME_PATTERNS table (theme × language regexes) plus a papal / CST document catalog. Collection keywords are broader harvest gates, separate from binary scoring patterns.

ActionGeo ≠ viewership

GDELT ActionGeo is an event-location field: where the reported action or writing desk was geocoded into the stream: often a national wire hub: not who read or watched the story. That is why Washington, D.C. dominates metro ranks. Places heat and city dots are therefore best read as writing geography, while country lifts compare theme density across ActionGeo nations under that same constraint.

Corpus coverage

News is the densest layer (2015–Q2 2026). TV, radio, and YouTube are present but thinner; a social-media (X) layer is planned. Public demo shards use the privacy pipeline above.

Limits & prototype status

This site is initial public development. Lexicon matches are high-precision patterns, not full semantic parsers; English-language wire volume still skews hemispheric totals; ActionGeo confounds “where faith is lived” with “where desks write.” Findings will deepen as gold-standard annotation, multimodal balance, and peer review land: the interface is built so those updates remain inspectable by students and pastors alike.
