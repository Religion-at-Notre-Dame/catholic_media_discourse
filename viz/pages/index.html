Core research question

What do learners already know, assume, or misunderstand about Catholicism from news and digital media, and how do country and language shape that informal learning before OCIA or parish formation?

How this prototype breaks it down

  1. Prior environments Which themes circulate in media before formal instruction, and where do lifts vs. a U.S. baseline appear by ActionGeo country and metro?
  2. Language How do parallel English, Spanish, and Portuguese lexicon teachings travel as one concept? Inside television and YouTube, Spanish excerpts are flagged more often than English ones. News in this set is almost all English, and Portuguese is too small to test. Language
  3. Accuracy triage How can multi-label categories and computational accuracy scores help flag misunderstandings for educator review? About 3% of excerpts are flagged. At paragraph level news is higher than television, YouTube, and radio. After grouping by outlet and day, television rises to news and radio stays lower. Doctrine is flagged far more often than institutional news. Triage Findings Misconceptions Methods
  4. Education How do findings translate into diagnostics, media literacy, and multilingual resources, without mistaking writing location for audiences?

The gap

Official documents are not the whole conversation

People often meet Catholicism in news and digital media before a classroom, yet analysis still stays in U.S. English sources or magisterial texts alone. Latin American audiences spend nearly double the global average on online news, so a hemispheric media view is required, not one English register.

Theological accents · Notre Dame

Gustavo Gutiérrez and liberation theology

The preferential option for the poor is not only a theme in theology. It leaves a measurable footprint in public media.

Gustavo Gutiérrez, founder of liberation theology and a Notre Dame presence, centered that option. Early news results already show significantly higher liberation-theology prevalence in Latin America than in U.S. coverage, a sign that theological accents circulate differently by country and language.

That contrast supports a public-language reading of Gutiérrez: the option leaves a footprint in news by writing location. It does not show the theology is practiced more, or that one media voice covers the hemisphere. Which claims the tests can touch
