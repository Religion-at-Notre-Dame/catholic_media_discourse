Formation · accuracy scale

Common misconceptions, by how far they drift

Two different measures sit on this page. The misunderstanding flag is a content label, about 3% of excerpts. Grouping paragraphs from the same outlet and day keeps a news-versus-radio gap and lifts television up to news. The accuracy score is separate: most excerpts are “cannot assess.”

Measured · news versus radio

News is highest. Radio is the tested contrast.

Same test after grouping outlet and day

Paragraphs from the same outlet on the same day are one group. The group is flagged if any paragraph in it is flagged. That is the dependence check.

Where the excerpt came from

Outlet is not the same as content type

An outlet is the stream: news, television, YouTube, or radio. Institutional news is a content label that can appear inside any of those streams.

What the labels mean

A passage can carry more than one type

Shares are of the labeled excerpts. They do not add to 100%, because one paragraph can carry two types. “Institutional news” is not a newspaper section. It means the excerpt is about a Church office, appointment, event, or schedule, and it does not teach a doctrine.

How wrong, when the model scores it

Clearly inaccurate, partial, or not a teaching

Score 1 is clearly inaccurate: the model reads the claim as contradicting ordinary Catholic teaching. Score 2 is partial or misleading: something true is missing or skewed. Score 0 is cannot assess, not a verdict of error. Score 4, accurate and clear, does not appear in this run. These are triage scores, not a theologian’s judgment.

Where the flag is more common

Doctrine and moral teaching carry it most often

Each bar is the share of that content type also marked misunderstanding. Institutional news is common in the corpus and rarely flagged. Doctrine is uncommon and flagged much more often.

Same excerpt

The flag rides with doctrine, ethics, and opinion

Lift is how often two labels share an excerpt compared with chance. It is not a percent of the corpus.

Doctrine and sacraments

Creed, Mass, and what the Church actually claims to teach.

Partial or misleading

“The pope is infallible in everything he says.”

Infallibility is a narrow claim about defined teaching, not every interview or homily.

Clearly inaccurate

“Each Mass is a new sacrifice of Christ.”

The Church teaches one sacrifice of Christ, made present at Mass, not repeated as a second death.

Devotion and popular piety

Mary, saints, Guadalupe, rosary.

Partial or misleading

“Guadalupe is only a national symbol, not Marian devotion.”

The pilgrimage is cultural and religious. Dropping the devotion leaves a partial account.

Clearly inaccurate

“Catholics worship Mary as a goddess.”

The taxonomy’s own example of misunderstanding. Veneration is not worship due to God alone.

Moral teaching

Life, dignity, and what counts as Church teaching.

Partial or misleading

“Catholic social teaching is optional politics.”

CST is teaching about public life. A news fight about a bill is not the same thing as the principle.

Clearly inaccurate

“The Church teaches that some human lives count for less.”

That reverses the claim that every human life has dignity.

Papal documents and CST

Named encyclicals versus a paraphrase.

Partial or misleading

“Laudato Si’ is only about recycling.”

Care for the common home is in the text. Reducing it to one habit drops the moral argument.

Clearly inaccurate

“Fratelli Tutti replaces the Gospel with politics.”

The document is a papal exhortation on social friendship, not a substitute creed.

Migration and public controversy

A bishop’s statement is not always a doctrine.

Partial or misleading

“A bishop’s comment on a bill is defined dogma.”

Policy statements can teach prudentially. They are not the same act as a creed definition.

Clearly inaccurate

“The Church requires Catholics to vote for one party.”

Formation distinguishes moral teaching from a partisan instruction the Church does not give.
